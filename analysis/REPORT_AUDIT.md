# Full-workbook paper-trading audit — 26 September 2026

## Scope and method

I traversed **all 351 XLSX reports still present on this branch**, every sheet
(one per workbook) and every row, including trade rows and skipped/ghost disclosure
blocks. The row-by-row inventory is generated by `python analysis/audit_reports.py`
into `analysis/output/report_manifest.json` and `trade_rows.json`. There are
**12,416 trade rows**, 11 zero-trade reports, and no workbook parsing warnings.
These reports cover M1/M2/M5–M11 and the one-day M9 variants. M12–M14 reports
were deleted on the previous request and cannot be re-audited from the current
working tree; their earlier aggregate counts are recorded in `M15_AUDIT.md`.
Old versions remain accessible in Git history at the parent of commit fc2093c.

The sheets contain disclosure sections: 341 reports have skipped-signal
headings; 131 have ghost headings. These are **not executed trades** and are
excluded from win/P&L totals. Summary and decorative cells are also excluded.
Four early files (M1 and M2 on July 22–23) have **145 trade rows with no costs
or net P&L column**: they cannot be counted as net winners. Note that the
reports are model outputs, not broker fills. An `OPEN` exit field indicates a
mark-to-market valuation, not a confirmed completed exit. **5,908 rows** across
all models have that marker. Results from different models overlap substantially:
678 of M5's 756 net-labeled trades match M2 on day, stock, side and entry time;
they are not independent replications. 562 of those 678 also have identical net
values (others can differ through sizing/marking/report timing).

## Disclosure-block review

Skipped-signal blocks collectively headline **60,878 not-traded rows** across
reports (many are the same underlying signal repeated in multiple models).
The most frequent documented blockers are scanner-table previews without a
chart label (over 24,000 combined across differently worded reasons), weak
EX variants, early EX1/EX2 restrictions, and M11's pre-09:45 gate. A skipped
row is **not** a scored missed opportunity: its eventual exit and execution
cost cannot be inferred from the skip table alone. The 131 ghost sections are
shadow/counterfactual outputs, not entered positions, and should never be
pooled into the trade win rate. This separation is essential when testing any
new gate against the reports.

## Can chronological selection meet 1–3 per day?

For each model and report date, I sorted net-labeled trade rows by entry time,
then symbol as a deterministic *tie breaker*, and took the first N rows.
This is a retrospective approximation: a live process needs a predeclared,
observable ranking when many signals share a 09:30 timestamp. Alphabetical
stock order is arbitrary and not an edge. Also, the first two early M1/M2
reports lack net labels, so their days are excluded from these counts.

rate. Both models converge to nearly identical picks in the late period.

## Best *hypothesis*, not a validated model

M5's **SELL-EX8** cohort has 80 net-labeled rows with 42 winners and +₹4,822.
The first two chronological SELL-EX8 rows on days where they occurred gave
32/52 winners and +₹6,795 over 32 of M5's 44 traded days. Of these 52, **23
were OPEN marks**. By period, the first-two selection gave early 11/20 wins,
+₹880 (12 active days); middle 9/13, +₹3,045 (7 active days); late 12/19,
+₹2,871 (13 active days). This is a *candidate for forward falsification*, not
proof: the subset, cap and time windows were noticed after examining outcomes;
multiple-comparison bias is unavoidable. It also misses at least 12 days and
therefore does not meet a guaranteed daily trade minimum. SELL-EX3 first two
are more frequent (41/44 active days) but early results were -₹950 and the
late gains depend heavily on OPEN marks.

### Operational interpretation

The existing M5 gate (OI spurt + movers + breakout setup) may be a better
*starting point* than M15's failed-range-reclaim hypothesis. However, the
reports do **not** substantiate a stable 1–3-trades-per-day, very-high-accuracy
edge. Do not replace M15 or launch new alerts on the strength of a retrospectively
selected code. First reconcile OPEN rows with final 15:20 exits, reconstruct
**when alerts were actually emitted** rather than assuming the candle-close
price was executable, specify a live-safe simultaneous-signal ranking, and
paper-test a frozen rule on new sessions with costs, fill delay, daily coverage,
win rate and drawdown tracked. A rule that must force one trade on a no-signal
day cannot preserve a high-conviction threshold.
